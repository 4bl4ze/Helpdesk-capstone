DeskTrace Wireframes

CSM 399 Capstone · Group 16 · UI/UX (Obed Opoku Junior, Louis Mawulolo) · v1.0

How to read these

Ten screens covering all four actors. Phone frames are 340px wide (roughly a mid-range Android); desktop frames are the manager and asset-officer views. Everything is greyscale on purpose. A wireframe shown in the real palette gets reviewed as though it were finished — people argue about button shades and miss that the form is in the wrong order. The only colour on this page is the 1 review markers, which are annotations, not interface.

Each screen lists the proposal requirements it satisfies, so the set can be checked against section 4.2 directly.

  1. 1
    Arriving by QR pre-fills the device, category and location, and says so. The requester's job shrinks to one sentence of description. If they got here from the menu instead, this strip is replaced by a normal device picker. Change is present because a scan can be of the wrong label.
  2. 2
    Urgency is a segmented control showing each band's response target, not a dropdown. Two reasons. One tap instead of three. And — more importantly — a requester choosing "Critical" can see they are claiming a 15-minute response, which is the cheapest possible brake on urgency inflation. Every helpdesk drowns in self-declared Critical tickets; showing the promise at the point of choosing is a design answer to that.
  3. 3
    Photos are optional and visibly so. Three fixed slots rather than a file list, so the three-photo limit (FR-TKT-01) is expressed by the layout instead of by an error message after the fact.
  4. 4
    The duplicate warning appears before sending, not after. It warns and still lets them send — the same projector can genuinely fail twice. Phrased as a question of fact, not a scolding.
  5. 5
    Send is sticky at the bottom, inside thumb reach, and states the offline consequence in plain words. "Pending sync" means nothing to a lecturer; "sends when you are back online" does.
Satisfies FR-TKT-01, FR-OFF-01. Supports FR-AST-03 (pre-filled from scan) and the Could-have duplicate flag.

  1. 1
    Offline is a banner, not a toast. It persists while the condition persists. FR-OFF-02 asks for Offline / Syncing / Up to date — a toast that disappears cannot represent a state that lasts.
  2. 2
    An unsent ticket is dashed and has no reference number, because it does not have one yet. Showing a fake reference that later changes would undermine the whole audit trail. It says "Not sent yet" where a reference would go.
  3. 3
    A sent ticket is solid, with its real reference, assignee and countdown. Put side by side with the card above, the dashed-to-solid change is the entire sync story told visually — this is what the examiner sees in demo step 3.
  4. 4
    Confirm and reopen sit on the list card, not behind a tap into detail. Closing the loop is the action most likely to be skipped, so it costs one tap. "Not fixed — reopen" rather than "Reopen", because the requester is reporting a fact, not operating a state machine.

    The SLA block states the promise and the outcome in words, with the bar as reinforcement. A requester does not know what "81%" means; "2h over target" they understand immediately. The response and resolution clocks are reported separately because they are separate commitments (FR-SLA-01).

    Grouped by deadline, not just sorted by it. A sorted list still needs reading top to bottom to find where "late" ends.   1. 1
    Device history is at the top, above the fault report. This is the most important ordering decision in the whole set. The proposal's own scenario (§6.4) is a technician who "sees the projector failed twice this term with the same lamp error" — that insight is worthless if it is three scrolls down. Putting repair history first is what makes the asset register useful to a technician rather than just to an auditor.
  2. 2
    The requester's own words and photo come second, verbatim. Technicians distrust paraphrase; the photo often settles the diagnosis before arrival.
  3. 3
    "What did you check or change?" instead of "Notes". A labelled prompt produces usable history; an empty box labelled "Notes" produces "fixed". The quality of next term's repair history is decided by the wording of this one label. Part used is separate so parts can be counted later.
  4. 4
    Two actions, weighted. Resolve is twice the width of Pause because it is the common case. Pause states its consequence — FR-SLA-03 pauses the clock in Waiting, and a technician must understand they are stopping a measured commitment, not hiding a ticket.
Satisfies FR-TKT-03, FR-SLA-03, FR-AST-03.

  1. 1
    Four counts, then two averages. The counts answer "what is happening now", the averages answer "how are we doing generally". Mixing them in one row of six makes a manager read all six to find the urgent one.
  2. 2
    Charts are deliberately small and side by side. They are for spotting a shape — one technician's bar twice everyone else's — not for reading values. Anything needing an exact number is a KPI tile or a table instead.

    The dashboard ends in actions, not figures. "Needs you now" lists the breached and at-risk tickets with Reassign on the row. A dashboard that only reports leaves the manager to go hunting in another tab for the thing it just alarmed them about.

What is not wireframed, and why

  • Licence tracker. A Should-have (FR-LIC-01) and a plain table — it reuses the asset register layout in S09 with three columns. No new thinking needed.
  • Settings / SLA target editing. The proposal lets the manager set targets, but §4.3 says the values are illustrative and tuned by agreement. A seeded config screen is not worth the build time before the core flows are solid.
  • Live camera scanning view. It is a viewfinder with a frame overlay; the design question is what happens after the scan, which is S09.
